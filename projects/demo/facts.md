# Facts: Why Are Airplanes Painted White? (`demo`)

Every factual claim in the narration, with a verdict and a source. Verdicts:
**true** (well supported), **plausible** (widely stated by industry sources, no hard study),
**misleading** (reworded in the script), **false/unproven** (corrected or cut).
Checked 2026-11-15.

## Claims from the storyboard

| # | Storyboard claim | Verdict | What the script says instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | White reflects solar radiation, protects materials, controls heat | **true** | Kept as the main reason (c02) |
| S2 | "White paint is lighter", saving fuel | **misleading** | Livery weight depends on layers and coverage, not colour; bare metal is lightest; polishing costs ate the savings (c03) |
| S3 | White saves maintenance cost | **plausible** | Framed as slower visible fading and fewer repaints (c03) |
| S4 | Cracks, dents and oil leaks are easier to spot on white | **plausible** | Kept, explicitly as a practical bonus that is hard to quantify (c04) |
| S5 | White helps birds see planes and avoid strikes | **unproven** | One correlational 2011 study, authors call it "suggestive"; script says it is not why airlines choose white (c05) |
| S6 | White makes leasing and rebranding easier | **true / plausible** | Kept; ~half the fleet is leased (c06) |

## Dropped or avoided

- "Concorde stretched 15–25 cm in flight": commonly repeated, not checked against a primary
  source in this pass, so left out.
- Exact litres of paint per aircraft: sources range from ~265 to ~3,600 L; too vague, left out.
- Specific fuel savings from white vs coloured paint: no reliable number exists; not claimed.
- "The new Air Force One keeps the light-blue livery": outdated since Feb 2026; corrected.
